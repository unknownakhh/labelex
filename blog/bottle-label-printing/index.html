Bottle Label Printing
for beverage brands

Labelex — Gauteng, South Africa
12 Jul 2026
9 min read

A beverage label has a harder job than almost any other product label. It has to survive the cold chain — condensation in the fridge, immersion in an ice bucket, handling with wet hands — and still look sharp on the shelf against imported competition. Bottle label printing is where substrate, adhesive, format and finish all have to be specified correctly before a single label is printed, because the environment a beverage bottle lives in is unforgiving of the wrong choice. This guide walks beverage brand owners and procurement managers through the format decision, the substrates and waterproof adhesives that work, how to match labels to wine, beer, spirit, water and soft drink bottles, and the South African liquor labelling rules that govern what has to appear on the label.

Why Bottle Labels Are Different to Every Other Product Label

Most product labels live a fairly gentle life — they sit on a dry shelf, get handled a few times, and are read once. Beverage bottle labels live in the opposite world. Between production and the customer's hand, a bottle label is refrigerated, sweated on, dunked in ice water, and gripped with wet fingers. The label that survives all of that and still sells the product is the result of getting three things right at specification stage.

The cold chain and condensation

The single biggest difference with bottle labels is refrigeration. When a chilled bottle is taken out of a fridge, condensation forms across the entire surface within seconds. A wine bottle sits in an ice bucket for an hour. A beer bottle is pulled out of melting ice at a braai. This constant wet-and-dry cycling is exactly what makes ordinary paper labels curl at the edges, lift, wrinkle or discolour. Bottle labels need a substrate and adhesive combination specifically rated for condensation and immersion — this is the non-negotiable baseline of beverage label printing.

Glass and PET surface adhesion

Beverage bottles are mostly glass or PET plastic, and these surfaces behave differently to the cardboard, HDPE and metal that other products use. Glass is easy to bond to with the right permanent adhesive, but the moment condensation gets between the label and the glass, a weak adhesive fails. PET bottles have a lower surface energy and can be trickier, especially on tight curves where the label wants to spring back off the bottle. The adhesive has to be matched to the specific container material, not assumed.

Shelf competition and premium signalling

Beverages are one of the most visually competitive retail categories in South Africa. A local wine, craft beer, gin or sparkling water sits on the shelf directly alongside imported and established brands. The label is the primary signal of quality tier, and beverage buyers make snap judgements on it. This is why textured papers, foil stamping and tactile finishes are so common in beverage label printing — they do the premium-positioning work that the liquid inside cannot do through glass.

Label Formats for Bottles: Pressure-Sensitive vs Shrink Sleeve vs Wrap-Around

Before substrate or finish, the first decision in any bottle label order is the format. There are three main ways to decorate a beverage bottle, and the right one depends on the bottle shape, the coverage you want, and the positioning of the brand. Getting this decision right early saves reworking artwork later.

Format Best for Coverage Premium finishes
Pressure-sensitive (self adhesive) Wine, spirits, craft beer, premium water Front & back panels Widest range
Heat shrink sleeve Contoured bottles, full-body designs, tamper bands Full 360° incl. neck Good
Wrap-around Water, soft drinks, high-volume cylindrical bottles Full circumference Moderate

Pressure-sensitive labels

Pressure-sensitive — also called self adhesive — labels are the default for most beverage bottles where a defined front-and-back label is the design. They offer the widest choice of premium substrates and finishes, which is why they dominate wine, spirits and craft categories. The label is die-cut to any shape, printed with the full range of finishes, and applied to the bottle with a permanent waterproof adhesive. For most beverage brands, this is the format to start with.

Heat shrink sleeves

Heat shrink sleeves are printed film tubes that are slipped over the bottle and shrunk with heat to conform tightly to the container shape — including complex curves and the neck. They are the right choice when you want full 360-degree decoration, a tamper-evident neck band, or when the bottle shape is too contoured for a flat label to sit cleanly. Sparkling and contoured beverage bottles frequently use shrink sleeves for this reason.

Wrap-around labels

Wrap-around labels cover the full circumference of a cylindrical bottle in a single continuous label. They are the standard format for water bottles, soft drinks and high-volume beverages where a clean, full-wrap graphic is wanted at low unit cost. Clear BOPP film is commonly used here to create the popular no-label look, where the design appears printed directly onto the bottle and the water or liquid inside is fully visible.

Substrates and Waterproof Adhesives for Beverage Bottles

The substrate is the label material itself; the adhesive is what holds it to the bottle. For beverage labels these two choices are inseparable, because a beautiful substrate with the wrong adhesive fails in the fridge, and a durable adhesive under the wrong substrate looks cheap on shelf. Here are the combinations that work.

Textured and cotton wine papers

Premium wine labels are traditionally printed on textured, felt-marked or cotton-based papers that carry a tactile, high-quality feel. These stocks signal craft and heritage, and they take foil stamping and embossing beautifully. Because a wine bottle spends time in a cold, damp environment, the premium paper is paired with a wet-strength treatment or a waterproof laminate and an adhesive rated for cold, moist conditions so the label stays intact in a wine fridge or ice bucket.

White and clear BOPP film

BOPP (Biaxially Oriented Polypropylene) film is the workhorse substrate for beverages that face the most water. White BOPP is durable, waterproof and tear-resistant — ideal for beer, cider and RTD (ready-to-drink) bottles. Clear BOPP creates the no-label look used by premium water, gin and vodka brands. Both survive immersion and condensation without the substrate itself breaking down, which paper cannot always guarantee.

Wet-strength paper

Wet-strength paper is treated to hold together when saturated, which is what allows a paper-look label to survive an ice bucket. It is widely used for beer and cider where the brand wants a paper aesthetic but the bottle lives in ice. Critically, wet-strength paper is often paired with a wash-off adhesive for returnable bottles, or a permanent waterproof adhesive for single-use bottles — the choice depends on whether the bottle is returned and re-filled.

Adhesive systems for the cold chain

  • Permanent waterproof adhesive: the standard for single-use beverage bottles — bonds through condensation and immersion and stays put for the life of the bottle
  • Ice-bucket / all-temperature adhesive: rated to apply and hold at low temperatures, for bottles that are chilled or frozen through the supply chain
  • Wash-off adhesive: for returnable glass bottles that go through industrial caustic washing and re-filling, where the old label must release cleanly
  • Deep-freeze adhesive: for products stored below zero, where standard adhesives lose grip

Matching Labels to Bottle Types

Each beverage category places its own demands on the label — driven by how the bottle is stored, chilled, handled and positioned. Here is how the most common South African beverage bottles map to label decisions.

Wine bottles

Wine labels prioritise premium feel and shelf presence. Textured or cotton paper with foil stamping and embossing is the norm, applied as a front-and-back pressure-sensitive set. The adhesive must handle a wine fridge and ice bucket without edge lift, and the artwork must carry the vintage, cultivar and origin information that the Liquor Products Act requires. Wine label stickers are one of the most finish-intensive beverage categories, and small production runs are common for boutique estates and single-vineyard bottlings.

Beer and cider bottles

Beer labels have to survive ice. Wet-strength paper or white BOPP with a waterproof or ice-bucket adhesive is the standard, because bottles routinely sit in melting ice for hours. Craft beer in particular leans on bold, characterful label designs to stand out in a crowded fridge, and short-run digital printing suits the frequent new-release and seasonal cadence that craft breweries run.

Spirits — gin, vodka, whisky

Spirit bottles are a premium showcase. Clear BOPP for the no-label look, metallic and textured substrates, heavy foil stamping and spot UV are all common. Gin in particular has driven a wave of premium local bottling in South Africa, and the label is the entire brand story on a clear or coloured glass bottle. These labels combine the durability requirements of a beverage with the finish expectations of a luxury product.

Water bottles

Water bottle labels are usually clear or white BOPP applied as a wrap-around on a PET bottle. The priority is condensation resistance and low unit cost at high volume. Clear BOPP delivers the clean, minimal no-label look that premium still and sparkling water brands favour, while white BOPP carries a full graphic where the brand wants colour and coverage.

Soft drinks and RTDs

Carbonated soft drinks, energy drinks and ready-to-drink beverages typically use either wrap-around pressure-sensitive labels or full heat shrink sleeves. Shrink sleeves are popular here because they allow full-body decoration on contoured bottles and can carry vibrant, edge-to-edge graphics that stand out in the fridge. Both formats hold up to condensation and repeated refrigeration.

Finishes That Sell Beverages on Shelf

On a beverage shelf, the finish is often the difference between a bottle that reads as premium and one that reads as budget. The same printed design shifts price tier entirely depending on the finish treatment applied.

Foil stamping

Hot foil stamping applies metallic gold, silver, copper or rose gold to specific elements — the brand name, a crest, a vintage. It is the dominant premium signal in wine and spirits and is impossible to replicate with print alone. Foil catches light on the shelf and does the luxury-positioning work instantly.

Embossing and textured stock

Embossing raises design elements off the label surface, and textured papers add a tactile quality the customer feels the moment they pick up the bottle. Combined, they communicate craft and heritage — which is why they are so common on wine and premium spirit labels. The in-hand experience is part of what justifies a higher price point.

Spot UV and tactile varnish

Spot UV applies a high-gloss varnish to selected elements against a matte background, creating contrast and drawing the eye. It is a cost-effective way to add visual hierarchy and premium feel without the expense of foil, and it works especially well on modern craft beer and RTD labels.

The clear no-label look

Printing on clear BOPP so the design appears to float directly on the glass — with the liquid fully visible behind it — is the signature look of premium water, gin and vodka. Achieving it cleanly requires a white-ink underlay on any element that needs to read as opaque, which is a print-stage decision to confirm at quote.

What South African Law Requires on a Beverage Label

Beverage labels — and alcoholic beverage labels in particular — carry mandatory information under South African law. Getting these elements into the artwork before production avoids the most expensive mistake in bottle labelling: a full run rejected because a required declaration is missing.

Alcoholic beverages

Labels for wine, beer, spirits and other alcoholic beverages must display the product description, the alcohol content by volume, the net contents in metric units, the name and address of the producer or distributor, and the country of origin. The mandatory government health warnings on alcohol consumption must also appear on the label. Wine carries additional requirements around vintage, cultivar and origin claims under the Liquor Products Act, which are verified for products making estate or single-vineyard claims.

Non-alcoholic beverages

Water, soft drinks, juices and other non-alcoholic beverages fall under the Foodstuffs, Cosmetics and Disinfectants Act and its labelling regulations. Mandatory information includes the product name, net contents, ingredient list, nutritional information where required, the manufacturer or distributor details, and a batch code and best-before date. Any nutritional or health claims are regulated and must be substantiated.

Barcodes and batch coding

Almost every retail beverage needs a scannable barcode, and the label artwork must reserve clean, correctly-sized space for it with adequate quiet zones so it scans reliably at till. Batch numbers and best-before or bottling dates are usually applied inline at the production line via overprint, but the space and format for them have to be designed into the label from the start.

Compliance reality check: The most common cause of a rejected beverage label run is not print quality — it is a missing mandatory declaration, an unscannable barcode, or the government health warning left off an alcohol label. Confirm the full compliance checklist for your beverage category before signing off artwork, and it never becomes a reprint.

How to Brief a Bottle Label Order

An accurate quote and a right-first-time run depend on a complete brief. The manufacturer cannot specify the correct adhesive or proof the artwork for what was not provided. Here is what your label manufacturer needs.

What to provide at brief stage

  • Bottle specifications: material (glass or PET), shape, dimensions and diameter, and a physical sample where possible
  • Storage and chilling: whether the bottle is refrigerated, iced, frozen or returnable — this drives the adhesive choice
  • Format: pressure-sensitive front-and-back, wrap-around, or heat shrink sleeve
  • Label dimensions: width and height, and whether it is a single label or a front-and-back set
  • Substrate and finish: textured wine paper, BOPP, clear film; foil, emboss, spot UV — or describe the positioning and let the manufacturer recommend
  • Compliance elements: confirm the artwork carries the mandatory declarations for your beverage category and a correct barcode
  • Quantity and timeline: order and reorder volumes, and any retailer or seasonal deadline

Pre-production approvals

Before any beverage label goes into production at Labelex, you receive a digital proof at full size with all finishes specified. For premium runs — foil, emboss, textured stock — we can supply pre-production samples on the actual substrate so you can hold the physical label and see the finish before committing to the full run. On a wine or spirit launch, where the label is the brand, this step is almost always worth the few extra days.

Common Questions Beverage Brands Ask

What is the best label material for beer and wine bottles?
For beer bottles that sit in ice buckets and fridges, a wet-strength or synthetic substrate with a permanent waterproof adhesive is the standard — it survives condensation and ice-water immersion without the label lifting or the paper disintegrating. For wine bottles, textured or cotton-based premium papers are the norm because they signal quality on shelf, paired with an adhesive rated for the cold, damp conditions of a wine fridge. The right combination depends on how the bottle is stored and chilled.
Should I use pressure-sensitive labels or shrink sleeves for my bottles?
Pressure-sensitive (self adhesive) labels suit most wine, spirit and premium beverage bottles where a defined front-and-back label is the design, and they offer the widest range of premium substrates and finishes. Heat shrink sleeves suit bottles with complex curves, contoured shapes or where a full 360-degree wrap and tamper-evident neck band is needed. Most beverage brands use pressure-sensitive; shrink sleeves are chosen for specific shapes and full-coverage designs.
Do bottle labels need a waterproof adhesive?
In almost all cases, yes. Beverage bottles face condensation from refrigeration, immersion in ice buckets, and handling with wet hands. A standard paper adhesive will lift, curl or discolour under these conditions. A waterproof or ice-bucket-rated permanent adhesive keeps the label bonded and legible through the full cold chain from production to point of sale to the customer's fridge.
What is the minimum order quantity for bottle label printing in South Africa?
At Labelex our minimum order value is R3,000 across all label formats, including bottle labels. There is no fixed unit minimum — a short run of premium foil-stamped wine labels can qualify, as can a longer run of standard beer or water bottle labels. R3,000 in production value is the gate, not unit count, which makes short-run and craft beverage production viable.
Can you print labels for water bottles and soft drinks?
Yes. Water bottle labels are typically printed on clear or white BOPP film with a wrap-around format, and soft drink and carbonated beverage bottles often use either wrap-around pressure-sensitive labels or full heat shrink sleeves. Both formats resist condensation and refrigeration, and clear BOPP creates the popular no-label look for still and sparkling water brands.
What does South African law require on an alcoholic beverage label?
Alcoholic beverage labels in South Africa must display the product description, alcohol content by volume, net contents in metric units, the name and address of the producer or distributor, country of origin, and the mandatory government health warnings on alcohol consumption. Wine has additional requirements around vintage, cultivar and origin claims under the Liquor Products Act. Building these mandatory elements into the artwork before production avoids rejected runs.
